Why this loan

What makes the Fixed-Rate Mortgage different.

Fixed-rate mortgages remove interest-rate risk from the equation. If you plan to keep the home for years, this is usually the right call.

1
Rate locked at closingYour interest rate is set at the moment your loan funds. Market rates can rise 5% and your payment does not change.
2
Identical principal and interest paymentEvery month for the life of the loan, the P&I portion of your payment is the same. Tax and insurance escrows may adjust.
3
Multiple term optionsChoose 15, 20, 25, or 30 years. Shorter terms build equity faster at a slightly lower rate.
4
No prepayment penaltyMake extra payments whenever you want. Pay the loan off early without penalty on any qualified mortgage.
5
Refinance always an optionIf rates drop, refinance into a lower fixed rate. Your monthly payment never automatically adjusts up.
6
Predictable budgetingEvery household budgeting tool works smoothly with a fixed-rate mortgage. No reset risk.
7
Assumable on government loansFHA, VA, and USDA fixed-rate mortgages can be assumed by qualified buyers when you sell, a major asset in a high-rate market.
8
Wide investor poolFixed-rate mortgages are the most competitively priced product in the market because they trade on the deep, liquid mortgage-backed securities market.

Is this loan right for you?

Fixed-rate loans suit borrowers who value certainty over short-term rate savings. Use this checklist.

  • You plan to keep the home for 7+ years
  • You want a predictable payment that does not change month-to-month
  • You prefer simplicity over the complexity of an ARM
  • You are buying at a time when rates are flat or expected to rise
  • Your household budget is sensitive to payment fluctuations
  • You want the option to refinance down (without ever being forced to absorb a rate increase)

Common Questions

What borrowers ask most.

Should I take a 15 or a 30-year fixed?
15-year fixed has a lower rate and builds equity faster, but the payment is much higher. 30-year fixed has the lowest payment but higher lifetime interest. We model both.
What happens if rates drop after I close?
Refinance. The fixed-rate loan only locks you in if you do nothing. There is no penalty for paying it off or refinancing it.
Why is the fixed rate higher than the ARM rate?
The lender takes the interest-rate risk for 30 years. The ARM passes that risk to you after the initial fixed period. The fixed-rate premium is the price of certainty.
Can I make extra payments on a fixed-rate loan?
Yes. There is no prepayment penalty on any qualified residential mortgage. Extra principal payments shorten the loan and save interest.
Will my fixed payment ever change?
The principal-and-interest portion never changes. Your full monthly payment can change if property taxes or homeowner's insurance go up, because those are held in escrow.
