Why this loan

What makes the Reverse Mortgage different.

Reverse mortgages are powerful tools when used correctly. We explain the obligations as clearly as the benefits.

1
Tax-free loan proceedsFunds received from a reverse mortgage are not considered taxable income by the IRS.
2
No monthly mortgage payment requiredYou must continue paying property taxes, insurance, and HOA, but no mortgage P&I payment is required.
3
Stay in your homeYou retain ownership of the home and live there as long as you wish, provided you meet the obligations.
4
Federally insured (HECM)Home Equity Conversion Mortgages are FHA-insured, which guarantees you or your heirs will never owe more than the home is worth.
5
Choose how you receive fundsLump sum, monthly tenure payments, line of credit, or any combination. You decide.
6
Line of credit grows over timeUnused HECM line of credit grows at the loan rate, increasing your available funds the longer you wait to draw.
7
Non-borrowing spouse protectionsA spouse under 62 can remain in the home if the borrowing spouse passes away, under specific HUD protections.
8
HUD counseling requiredAn impartial HUD-approved counselor reviews the program with you before you commit. We pay for it.

Is this loan right for you?

Reverse mortgages fit very specific situations. Use this checklist honestly before pursuing one.

  • All borrowers on title are 62 or older
  • Your home is your primary residence (no rentals, no second homes)
  • You have substantial equity, typically 50% or more
  • You can continue paying property taxes, insurance, and HOA
  • You plan to stay in the home long enough to amortize the upfront costs
  • You have spoken with adult children and other heirs about the program's effect on inheritance

Common Questions

What borrowers ask most.

Do I still own my home with a reverse mortgage?
Yes. You retain title and ownership. The lender places a lien for the loan balance, identical to any other mortgage.
What happens when I pass away?
Heirs can pay off the loan and keep the home, sell the home and keep the equity, or deed the home to the lender. They never owe more than the home is worth.
Can I owe more than my home is worth?
No. HECMs are non-recourse, meaning you and your heirs are never personally liable for any shortfall.
Are reverse mortgages a scam?
No, but they are sometimes mis-sold. We focus on borrower fit and on transparent disclosure of upfront costs, ongoing obligations, and effects on heirs.
What does a reverse mortgage cost upfront?
Origination fees, FHA mortgage insurance premium (2% upfront, financed), appraisal, and counseling. Typically 5% to 7% of the home value in total, financed into the loan.
